# Making the golf humanoid simulation realistic and visually great

Scratch research and prototype, written 2026-10-16. **Nothing here is committed or pushed, and no repo env files were edited.**
All prototype code, outputs and videos are in `<local-path>/sim_proto/` (outside the repo). The repo's `env/` files were not touched.

Everything below was run on this box with MuJoCo 3.16. Where a number comes from my own prototype it is labelled **[proto]**. Where it comes from a source I opened it is labelled **[src]**. Things I recall from background knowledge and did not verify are labelled **[unverified]**.

---

## 0. TL;DR

1. **Offscreen rendering works on this box, but only through `xvfb-run` plus GLFW with Mesa software rendering (llvmpipe).** `MUJOCO_GL=egl` and `MUJOCO_GL=osmesa` both fail, because libEGL and libOSMesa are not installed. Cost: about 0.2 s per frame at 1280x720 with 8x MSAA, so a 30 s video renders in 2–3 minutes. No GPU is needed.
2. **A ~30 s showcase clip exists.** It has a textured golf hole (fairway stripes, bunkers, pond, 300 trees, flag), three camera moves, slow motion, a clubhead trail, a ball tracer, and a launch-monitor overlay. It is `<local-path>/sim_proto/out/golf_showcase_demo.mp4`. There is also a 132 mph power-swing variant.
3. **The current repo physics model is the real bottleneck, not RL.** My prototype athlete has real anthropometrics (1.85 m, 85 kg, segment lengths and masses from published tables). It has a two-handed grip, a proper 0.325 kg club, and a realistic shaft. The same physics reaches 107–132 mph club speed when it is choreographed, versus about 0.2 m/s in the RL checkpoints.
4. **The scripted swing is kinematic.** The prototype does not yet *learn* to swing. Its joint torques exceed human limits in the arms (see §4), so the next step is trajectory optimization or RL under real torque limits.
5. **Use an analytic impact and ball-flight model for training and eval metrics.** I calibrated it to TrackMan PGA Tour driver averages. MuJoCo soft-contact ball launch was fine on speed (about +8% versus analytic), off on launch angle (+3–4°), and **wrong on spin** (it gave topspin). Use MuJoCo contact for visuals only until it is tuned.
6. **Roadmap to a full course** is in §5: multi-club bag, shot shaping, terrain, wind, a shot-selection layer, and strokes-gained as the top-level metric.

## 1. Better humanoid body

### 1.1 Anthropometrics (implemented: `anthro.py`)

Segment lengths use Winter-style fractions of stature, so any athlete height can be generated by changing `H`. Masses use de Leva / Dempster-style fractions of body mass. **[unverified]**: I used these from memory, not from a source opened today. Check against the original tables before publishing.

| Segment | Length (fraction of H) | At H = 1.85 m | Mass (fraction of M) | At M = 85 kg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| thigh | 0.245 | 0.453 m | 0.1416 | 12.0 kg |
| shank | 0.246 | 0.455 m | 0.0433 | 3.7 kg |
| foot (length / ankle height) | 0.152 / 0.039 | 0.281 / 0.072 m | 0.0137 | 1.2 kg |
| trunk, hip to shoulder centre | 0.288 | 0.533 m | chest 0.224 + abdomen 0.095 + pelvis 0.112 | 38 kg |
| upper arm / forearm / hand | 0.186 / 0.146 / 0.108 | 0.344 / 0.270 / 0.200 m | 0.0271 / 0.0162 / 0.0061 | 2.3 / 1.4 / 0.5 kg |
| head and neck | n/a | n/a | 0.0694 | 5.9 kg |
| shoulder width | 0.259 | 0.48 m | | |

**[proto]** The generated model weighs 84.8 kg including the club. It has 37 DOF: 13 per leg-or-arm pair as hinges (hip abduction/flexion/rotation, knee, ankle, ankle inversion, and shoulder x3, elbow, forearm rotation, wrist x2), plus pelvis yaw, spine pitch/yaw/roll, and head yaw. A golf stance is built in: 22° hip flexion, 18° knee flexion, 14° leg abduction, 38° trunk bend, 6° spine side-bend. The feet are flat and about 0.61 m apart, ankle to ankle.

Gaps I did not close:
- There is no fingers or scapula model.
- The pelvis is a separate yaw body rather than a lumbar twist.
- Legs are static, so there is no weight shift or ground reaction force.

### 1.2 Joint limits and actuators

- **Limits** are anatomical (for example elbow 0–150°, wrist flexion −85..90°, spine yaw −110..125°) and are in the generated XML.
- **Peak torque limits** are in `anthro.TORQUE`, and the generated XML has one `<motor>` per joint with `ctrlrange`/`forcerange` set to them. Examples: knee 130/240 Nm, hip flex 200/260, trunk yaw 150, shoulder flex 95/110, elbow 75/60, wrist flexion 22/28.
  - **[unverified]** These are my compiled athletic-male figures. They are not copied from one paper.
  - **[src]** For comparison, golf forward-dynamics models use torso rotation torque up to 136–164 Nm (literature max 192), shoulder 85–91 Nm, and wrist about 90 Nm (MacKenzie 2009 and a later predictive model, via search). Nesbit's inverse dynamics gave peak wrist "alpha" torque of 24–42 Nm.
  - So my arm and wrist limits are plausible, and the wrist limit may be a little low for a scratch golfer.
- **Recommended actuator model for RL:**
  1. Joint-space torque control (`<motor>`), because it keeps the policy honest about strength limits.
  2. Add a Hill-style torque–velocity derating. `anthro.WMAX` has per-joint max speeds for `tau = tau_max*(1 - w/w_max)`. MacKenzie's golf models show muscle-like torque falling with joint speed. I did not implement it as a callback; it can go in a small `mjcb_control` or in the env's pre-step clamp.
  3. PD position targets (`kp`, `kv`) are fine for imitation. Use `kv` high enough to avoid ringing at 0.5 ms timesteps. If the target is a mocap reference, tune the gear ratio so peak torque at peak speed is reachable.
- **A muscle-driven alternative exists:** MS-Human-700 (90 segments, 206 joints, 700 muscle-tendon units, Apache-2.0, in MuJoCo Menagerie, needs MuJoCo ≥ 3.2.6) **[src]**. It has locomotion and manipulation variants and `msgym` Gymnasium envs, but nothing golf-specific. It is far heavier to train. I recommend it as a long-term "biomechanics credibility" track, not for the next month. I did not run it.
- **Ground and feet:** foot geoms use friction 1.3, and ground 0.8. MuJoCo's default pyramidal cone is too slippery for a rotation-heavy swing, so use `cone="elliptic"` and `impratio=10` (set in the prototype). For golf shoes with spikes, consider friction 1.5–2.0 plus torsional friction. Real golfers also move their weight across the feet, which needs foot ground contact to be live. My prototype fixes the legs.
- **Grip:** the prototype welds the club to the right hand and solves the left hand by IK to a point 10 cm up the shaft. Options for the RL env:
  1. Rigid weld to both hands (simple, over-constrains).
  2. Weld right plus a soft `connect` equality on the left (`solref ~0.01`), so the left hand can slip and the arms do not fight.
  3. A real finger-wrap model, which is only worth it if you adopt MS-Human-700 hands.
  I recommend option 2 for training.

### 1.3 Check against real pro biomechanics

**[src]** Pro driver kinematic sequence: peak pelvis 456–477°/s, thorax 708–757°/s, lead arm 980–1055°/s, club 2000–2254°/s. Elite X-factor is about 55° at the top; thorax rotation at the top is about −92°, pelvis about −39° to −45°.

**[proto]** My choreography reproduces these in shape:

| Swing | Pelvis peak | Thorax peak | Club in-plane | Club speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "50" (target 50 m/s) | 388°/s | 594°/s | 2433°/s | 107 mph |
| "62" (power) | 479°/s | 731°/s | not recorded | 132 mph |

The 62 m/s swing reached 59 m/s, about 132 mph, not the 139 mph target. Both swings have a ~50° X-factor and the right proximal-to-distal ordering. These are targets for a style reward in training: the trainer can score "peak pelvis speed lead time versus thorax versus club".

## 2. A more realistic club and ball

### 2.1 Club

- Prototype `anthro.py` rigid club: 1.156 m total, head 200 g, shaft 75 g, grip 50 g, so 0.325 kg versus the repo's 0.78 kg shaft (from `research_driver_distance.md`). The head ellipsoid is a face box plus a lofted face of 10.5°. **The head is rotated relative to the shaft so the face looks down the target line at address with lie compensated.** Without that, loft about a tilted shaft gave a 5° closed face and a hook (I fixed this; the first run had a 45 m left miss).
- **Flexible shaft** (`club_flex.py`): 5 tapered segments of 0.15 m, each with two stiffness hinges (lead/lag and toe-droop), so it bends in two planes. Taper EI is 110 → 30 N·m² (butt to tip).
  - **[proto]** At the first-guess stiffness the first bending mode (grip clamped) is **7.0 Hz**. Scaling EI by 0.413 gives **4.5 Hz**, which is where a stiff-flex driver sits (target 4.2–4.6 Hz, **[unverified]**). Use `ei_scale=0.413`.
  - **[proto]** With the flexible club and the choreographed grip, head speed at impact is 48.2 m/s versus 48.2 m/s for a stiff club. The flexible head lags and leads by **16.7 mm** at impact.
  - So with this grip motion, flex changes impact position more than speed. Larger effects (lag/lead, a few m/s) need a physically driven swing, not a prescribed grip path.
- **COR / smash factor:** analytic impact uses COR 0.83 and 0.20 kg effective club mass, giving smash about 1.46–1.49 for a centered hit **[proto]**. TrackMan PGA Tour smash is 1.47–1.49 **[src]**.

### 2.2 Ball impact and flight (`ball_flight.py`)

- **Impact:** a two-body impact along the face normal, with a friction limit on the tangential part (µ = 0.22, roll-off limit `m*vt/3.5`). It gives backspin from the loft. It is deterministic and cheap.
- **Flight:** RK4 with drag, Magnus lift (`Cl = 0.42*S^0.4`, capped at 0.45), `Cd = 0.17 + 0.60*S` with a low-speed rise, spin decay of 3%/s, optional wind, then bounce and rolling resistance. Here `S = r*omega/v`.
  - **[src]** Real balls have Cd about 0.2–0.25 at high speed rising to about 0.5 at low speed, with Cl rising with spin factor (Bearman and Harvey 1976; Smits and Smith 1994 add a spin-decay law and Reynolds-dependent drag).
- **Calibration, [src] + [proto]:** TrackMan 2023–24 PGA Tour driver average is 115 mph club, 171 mph ball, 10.4° launch, 2545 rpm, 282 yd carry, ~35 yd max height. My model reproduces 171 mph, 10.4°, 2545 rpm as **275.9 yd carry, 100 ft apex, 6.7 s hang, 303 yd total**. Sensitivities: **+10 mph ball speed gives +19 yd carry** and **+1000 rpm spin gives −9 yd**. The second one is a rough rule of thumb I targeted (about −12), not a validated figure.
  - Five parameters were fit on one data point plus two rule-of-thumb sensitivities, so treat it as a calibrated toy, not a validated model. The earlier research doc quoted a 248.5 vs 251 m single-point fit from another source; this one is the same kind of evidence.
- **MuJoCo soft contact versus the analytic model** (same flexible-head kinematics; ball 46 g, face box; contact `solref -1e7 -300`, timestep 1e-5 near impact; `club_flex.py`):

  | | Ball speed | Launch | Backspin |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Analytic impact | 69.9 m/s | 11.1° | +3463 rpm |
  | MuJoCo contact (stiffness 1e7–3e7, damping 30–300) | 74–81 m/s | 13.6–15.2° | **−830 to −930 rpm (topspin)** |

  Speed is within about 6–15%, launch is 3–4° high, and spin has the wrong sign. Likely causes are contact friction/cone settings and a face that is a box with no roll or bulge. `solref` stiffness below 3e6 gave garbage (ball barely moves). **Recommendation: use the analytic model for all reported metrics, and treat MuJoCo contact as visuals-only until someone tunes friction, condim and face geometry.**
- **350-yard reality check from the model [proto]:** best total distance versus club speed, choosing launch (8–16°) and spin (1600–3400 rpm) freely, with smash 1.48. The best spin sits at the grid's lowest edge (1600 rpm), so the spin optimum is not trustworthy. Distances are probably a little optimistic.

  | Club speed | Best total (model) |
  |---|---|
  | 101 mph | 274 yd |
  | 113 mph | 313 yd |
  | 125 mph | 347 yd |
  | 134 mph | 370 yd |
  | 145 mph | 397 yd |

  So **about 125–130 mph club speed with launch around 14–16° and low spin is where a 350 yd total becomes plausible in this model.** TrackMan's PGA average (115 mph) gives about 300 yd total **[src]**.

## 3. Rendering a great showcase video

### 3.1 What works on this box

| Backend | Result |
|---|---|
| `MUJOCO_GL=egl` | **fails**: `NoneType has no attribute eglQueryString` (no libEGL) |
| `MUJOCO_GL=osmesa` | **fails**: no libOSMesa |
| `MUJOCO_GL=glfw` **inside `xvfb-run`** | **works** (Mesa llvmpipe software GL; GLFW from `pip install glfw`) |

Run command (from `<local-path>/sim_proto`):
```
MUJOCO_GL=glfw xvfb-run -a -s "-screen 0 1920x1080x24" venv/bin/python render_demo.py --out out/demo.mp4
```
Notes: set `<visual><global offwidth offheight>` at least as large as the render size. ffmpeg is installed (video is written by `imageio` + libx264, yuv420p). A venv at `<local-path>/sim_proto/venv` has mujoco, numpy, scipy, matplotlib, pillow, imageio, glfw (the existing `<local-path>/golfvenv` also has mujoco 3.16 but not glfw).

### 3.2 What the renderer does (`render_demo.py`, `scenery.py`)

- **Scenery:** procedural grass textures (mowing stripes, noise) via numpy and Pillow; fairway, tee box, green, flag, bunkers, a water pond with reflectance, yardage posts, tee markers, about 300 trees with foliage texture, a gradient skybox, and haze and fog. Ground is flat (physics-safe); the course is purely visual.
- **Cameras:** a free camera with per-shot paths: front orbit, a down-the-line replay, an impact close-up, a ball chase cam, and a landing/roll view. Each shot has its own speed curve.
- **Slow motion:** because the swing is stored at 1 kHz and interpolated, I can warp time freely. Speed is 0.40× through the backswing, ramping down to 0.06× at impact, 0.02× in the impact close-up, and 1× for flight.
- **Overlays:** the clubhead trail (cyan) is drawn as capsule geoms added to the scene. The ball tracer is orange capsules, plus a halo around the ball when it is far from the camera and a ring at the landing point. A launch-monitor panel shows live club speed, then locked ball speed, smash, launch, direction, backspin, sidespin, apex, carry (counting up live), total and offline. A top-view shot trace inset is drawn with Pillow.
- **Output:** `<local-path>/sim_proto/out/golf_showcase_demo.mp4` (29.9 s, 1280x720, 30 fps, 33 MB) and `golf_power_swing_132mph.mp4` (about 20 s, 21 MB).
- **Visible limitations:**
  - No real-time shadows showed up (a light/shadow setting I did not get to the bottom of).
  - The golfer is capsules and ellipsoids.
  - Some trees clip close to the landing camera.
  - Moving-ball spin is not drawn.
  - The ball is a plain sphere.

### 3.3 Ideas to push it further (cheap to expensive)

1. Fix shadows (try a spotlight, or `shadowsize` and `<light castshadow>` tuning) and add ambient occlusion by hand with a darker contact patch.
2. Motion blur by averaging 4–8 sub-frames at slow-motion times, and a depth-of-field blur in post.
3. 1920x1080 renders at about 3× the cost (still minutes).
4. A dimpled ball texture, a visible tee, and divot particles on impact.
5. A better body mesh: SMPL-style or Menagerie meshes retargeted to the same joints; clothes via textured geoms.
6. A heightfield fairway (`hfield`) for undulation; keep physics flat until terrain physics is added.
7. Match-cut a Mason reference swing next to the sim swing (side-by-side) once his references are retargeted.
8. Render from the true training policy rather than the choreography, as soon as one exists. That is the shot that matters.

## 4. Results of the prototype swing (inverse dynamics and ball flight)

All three swings are choreographed (min-jerk backswing, power-law downswing, exponential follow-through) with a swing-plane double-pendulum for the hands and club, and IK for both arms. They are **not** physically simulated or learned.

| Target | Club speed at impact | Ball speed | Smash | Launch | Backspin | Carry | Total | Offline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "40" | 86 mph | 126 mph | 1.46 | 8.4° | 2540 rpm | 162 yd | 196 yd | 6 yd left |
| "50" | 107 mph | 157 mph | 1.46 | 7.9° | 3139 rpm | 234 yd | 261 yd | 9 yd left |
| "62" | 132 mph | 193 mph | 1.46 | 7.7° | 3850 rpm | 296 yd | 319 yd | 14 yd left |

Impact conditions for "50": path −0.9° (slightly in-to-out), face-to-path +1.0° (slightly closed), attack −1.6°, dynamic loft 10.2°. Left is +y. Launch is low because the attack angle is negative; a real driver swing hits up by 1–4°, which the choreography does not do yet.

**Joint-torque check (inverse dynamics, `mj_inverse`, contacts and joint limits disabled).** Scaled to limits from `anthro.TORQUE`:

| Joint | Required peak ("50") | Limit | Ratio | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pelvis yaw | 66 Nm | 170 | 0.4× | fine |
| spine pitch | 357 | 300 | 1.2× | static bend holding gravity load; slightly high |
| spine roll | 237 | 180 | 1.3× | |
| spine yaw | 665 | 150 | 4.4× | far over; includes arm inertia |
| right shoulder flex / abd / rot | 372 / 671 / 226 | 110 / 90 / 75 | 3–7× | over |
| right elbow | 522 | 60 | 8.7× | far over |
| right wrist dev | 347 | 18 | 19× | at joint limit 54% of the time |
| left shoulder flex / abd | 47 / 68 | 95 / 90 | 0.5–0.8× | OK, but the left arm carries no club load in this model |

**Honest interpretation:** the arm and spine numbers are not reliable estimates of what a person needs. The IK chooses redundant arm solutions (shoulder and forearm rotation speeds up to 2,400–2,700°/s), and the model has the right hand carrying the whole club while the left hand just follows. **The correct conclusion is "the scripted arm motion is not humanly feasible as it stands", not "swinging needs 600 Nm".** Fixing this means optimizing trajectories or training an RL policy under the torque limits, with both hands loading the club. **[src]** Published golf models need about 85–91 Nm shoulder and 136–164 Nm torso torque for 100–110 mph-class swings.

Other caveats: the series is smoothed with a 6 ms Gaussian to remove IK jitter, and the IK residual at the right hand peaks at 3.5 cm ("50") and 12.8 cm ("62"). Total peak mechanical power was 7.5 kW ("50") and 10.2 kW ("62").

## 5. Roadmap to full course play (long term)

**Phase A: make the driver real (next 2–4 weeks).** Adopt the anthropometric body and the realistic club in the training env. Train the policy under the real torque limits with a swing-plane-gated reward: contact quality, then club speed, then face-to-path. Score with the analytic flight model. Keep the scripted choreography as a reference and a "teacher" for imitation. Use Mason's real references plus CMU mocap for style and sequencing.

**Phase B: the bag.** Generate clubs parametrically (length, mass, loft, lie, shaft EI taper, head inertia) from one table. TrackMan PGA Tour averages **[src]** give targets per club:

| Club | Club speed | Ball speed | Spin | Carry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Driver | 115 mph | 171 mph | 2545 rpm | 282 yd |
| 3-wood | 110 | 162 | 3663 | 249 |
| 5-wood | 106 | 156 | 4322 | 236 |
| 4-iron | 98 | 140 | 4782 | 209 |
| 7-iron | 92 | 123 | 7124 | 176 |
| Pitching wedge | 84 | 104 | 9316 | 142 |

The impact model needs per-club COR, loft and groove friction (more backspin for irons). Evaluate each club against its row above as a sanity check. Use one policy conditioned on a club embedding, or one policy per club family.

**Phase C: shot shaping and dispersion.** Make face angle, path and strike location controllable. Add off-center impacts with gear effect (toe and heel hits). Measure draw/fade curvature from sidespin. Report dispersion ellipses (the Evaluator already has `flight_tracker.py`).

**Phase D: environment.** Wind and air density (altitude, temperature); rough, fairway, bunker and green surface models with different restitution, friction and a drag penalty for a ball sitting in rough; uneven lies (ball above or below feet, uphill or downhill) via a heightfield and a rotated stance; water and out-of-bounds handled at the course layer. This is where the course art from §3 gets re-used as real terrain.

**Phase E: shot selection.** A hierarchical agent whose high-level policy picks club, target and shot shape and whose low-level swing policy executes it. Start with a model-based chooser: simulate dispersion for each candidate (club × aim × shape), score expected strokes from a lookup table, pick the minimum. Strokes gained (Broadie) is the natural top-level metric **[unverified]** and works well as the RL reward for the high-level layer.

**Phase F: course representation and metrics.** A hole as a graph (tee, fairway polygons, hazards, green with pin). Run 18 holes with a score, strokes-gained by category (off the tee, approach, around the green, putting), and "consistency over time" as session-to-session dispersion drift. Putting and short game are separate low-level skills with different physics (green speed/stimp, slope).

**Phase G: sim-to-real and Mason's data.** Retarget Mason's swings to the same skeleton, fit his segment lengths and masses, and compare his predicted ball flight with launch-monitor data if he has any. That would validate (or invalidate) the pipeline against a real person.

## 6. Concrete recommendations for the repo (without touching env files now)

1. Replace the club in the env with the 0.325 kg two-piece (shaft + head) model, or the flexible version with `ei_scale=0.413`. Use a head quaternion that compensates for lie (`swing.py` shows how).
2. Use `timestep` ≤ 5e-4 and `cone="elliptic"`. Turn on near-impact sub-stepping (1e-5) only if you keep MuJoCo ball contact.
3. Drive the ball with the analytic impact and flight model for all metrics, and keep MuJoCo contact for the render.
4. Add a torque-limit table and report "peak required torque / limit" in every eval, so unrealistic swings are visible.
5. Add a **club-speed-first curriculum reward** (club-speed gate, then face-to-path, then carry), with the choreography as a teacher.
6. Add the kinematic-sequence reward (pelvis → thorax → arm → club ordering and peak speeds) from the pro numbers in §1.3.
7. Reuse `render_demo.py` as the post-training showcase renderer; it accepts any `series_*.npz` with `t`, `Q`, `head`, `vel`, `ball`, `flight_*`, `meta`.
